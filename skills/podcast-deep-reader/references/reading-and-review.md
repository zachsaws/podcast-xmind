# Reading and semantic review

## Build a map that can retain side topics

Map the entire conversation before reorganizing. Each meaningful topic gets a
place, even if it does not serve the dominant narrative. The map can be brief;
the supporting explanation should preserve why the idea matters and how the
speaker arrived at it. Avoid one isolated bullet per claim as the whole report.

For each substantive section recover, where the source actually contains them:
the question, the speaker's answer, the mechanism or reasoning, specific
evidence or example, and limitations or dissent. Do not manufacture this
structure by filling absent elements. Say when an assertion has no evidence in
the episode. Keep contradictions legible and identify later self-corrections.
Repeated conclusions may merge; their distinct support and boundary conditions
must survive. A useful aside can be a short section rather than an appendix.

## Budget by information, not minutes alone

The scan layer should usually fit 2–3 minutes. A deep-read target of 15–25
minutes guides navigation, not forced expansion or truncation. Estimate roughly
350–500 Chinese characters/minute or 180–250 English words/minute, label the
assumption and adapt for formulas and technical material. A ten-minute episode
should not become a padded twenty-minute article. A dense two-hour conversation
may need a longer report, with a shorter optional path through it.

If the reader explicitly requests a strict short version, provide it as a
separate layer and name omitted topics. Do not silently change the retained
unit ledger to claim the shortened version is comprehensive.

## What deserves explicit checking

| Risk | Check |
|---|---|
| Attractive number | Exact value, currency, unit, period, denominator, comparison baseline and correction |
| Causal conclusion | What connects premise to outcome; source's reasoning vs editor's inference |
| Universal wording | Preserve “in our sample”, “may”, “not yet”, “if”, “approximately” and exceptions |
| Multi-speaker agreement | Who claims what; question, disagreement, joke and reported speech |
| Translation | Negation, conditionality, units, names; original excerpt for consequential ambiguity |
| ASR oddity | Keep uncertain spelling/quantity explicit; verify from audio only when available |
| Independent aside | Retain if it teaches a distinct useful idea, even outside the main theme |
| Source gap | State exactly what was acquired; never fill missing material with outside articles |

Fact/opinion/speculation labels are about the source's mode of assertion. For
example, “we served 80 people” is a reported fact, “this is the right strategy”
an opinion, and “we may launch next year” speculation. None becomes independently
verified simply because a transcript exists. External fact checks belong in a
separate box with their own source and check date.

## Two-way final review

First reread source segments without relying solely on the ledger; ask what a
reader would misunderstand if they read only the report. Then inspect the report
against evidence. Verify every critical/important unit and every quoted number,
speaker and translated claim. Spot-check additional optional material and every
uncertainty. Record actual findings in the run, not a prefilled “all checks passed”.

Report any unsupported leaps, missing topics and unresolved wording. Re-run the
structural audit after repairs. If a substantive problem remains, deliver the
material with a visible limitation rather than a clean-looking success label.
